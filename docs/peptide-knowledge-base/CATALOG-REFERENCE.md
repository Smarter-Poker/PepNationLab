## How To Read This Reference

Each compound is profiled with a consistent schema: class/type; identity (sequence, molecular
weight, CAS); mechanism and molecular target; research use cases; reported benefits with study
context; reported side effects; warnings/limitations; handling/storage quirks; regulatory status;
and key sources.

### Evidence-Strength Legend

- **Approved drug** -- FDA and/or EMA approved, robust human trial data.
- **Investigational** -- in active human clinical trials, not yet approved.
- **Preliminary/preclinical** -- evidence mainly animal or in-vitro, or limited single-group human data.
- **Research-chemical only** -- no approved human use; sold for laboratory research.
- **Cosmetic ingredient** -- recognized topical cosmetic active, not a drug.

### Anti-Doping Note

Many compounds here are on the WADA Prohibited List (growth-hormone secretagogues, GH/IGF agents,
TB-500, AICAR, MOTS-c, etc.). Anti-doping status is noted per compound. Status can change; verify
against the current WADA list before any competition context.

# 1. Anti-Aging & Longevity / Bioregulators

### Epithalon (Epitalon, Epithalone, AEDG)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic tetrapeptide bioregulator ("Khavinson peptide"); investigational geroprotector / telomerase modulator. Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG); C14H22N4O9; ~390.35 Da; CAS 307297-39-8. Minimal active fragment of the pineal extract epithalamin.
- **Mechanism / target:** Proposed to upregulate hTERT (telomerase catalytic subunit), increasing telomerase activity and telomere length in cultured human cells; binds DNA major groove (Khavinson model); reported to regulate melatonin and IL-2.
- **Research use cases:** Cellular senescence/telomere biology, geroprotection, neuroendocrine/circadian (melatonin) regulation, antioxidant/antimutagenic effects.
- **Reported benefits:** In vitro (human fibroblasts) -- induced hTERT, telomerase activity, telomere elongation (PubMed 12937682; confirmed Biogerontology 2025). Animal -- antioxidant/antitumor signals. Human -- limited, old, single-group (Khavinson) cohorts reporting reduced mortality; not independently replicated. Preliminary.
- **Side effects:** No well-characterized AE profile; reported well tolerated in sparse low-quality studies. Theoretical concern: telomerase upregulation could favor malignant-cell proliferation (unresolved vs reported antitumor effects).
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved anywhere; thin, non-independent human evidence. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized white powder; store frozen, dark, dry (-20C). Reconstitute with bacteriostatic/sterile water (acetic acid generally unneeded). Refrigerate solution; very short plasma half-life.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA/EMA approved; research chemical. Not specifically WADA-named (verify).
- **Sources:** pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12937682; link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10522-025-10315-x; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14523363; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitalon

### FOXO4-DRI (FOXO4 D-Retro-Inverso peptide)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic senolytic peptide (D-retro-inverso PPI inhibitor). Preclinical only.
- **Identity:** ~43-residue D-amino-acid, reverse-sequence analog of FOXO4 (protease-resistant); ~5 kDa; no consistent CAS. Targets FOXO4-p53 interaction.
- **Mechanism / target:** In senescent cells FOXO4 sequesters p53, blocking apoptosis. FOXO4-DRI disrupts FOXO4-p53, releasing p53 to trigger selective apoptosis of senescent cells. NMR shows binding to p53 transactivation domain (PubMed 40593617).
- **Research use cases:** Senolysis, aging/healthspan, chemotherapy-induced senescence recovery.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal (Cell 2017, Baar/de Keizer) -- selectively killed senescent cells; restored fur, renal function, fitness in aged/progeroid mice; countered doxorubicin toxicity. In vitro -- selective senescent-cell apoptosis. Human -- none. Preclinical only.
- **Side effects:** No human data. p53-activating apoptosis inducer: theoretical off-target apoptosis, injection-site reactions.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Entirely preclinical; mechanism risk uncharacterized in humans; costly/difficult synthesis. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; DRI design is unusually protease-resistant/stable. Store -20C dark/dry. Reconstitute sterile/bacteriostatic water; can be less soluble (length/charge). Refrigerate solution.
- **Regulatory:** Not approved; investigational/research chemical.
- **Sources:** cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(17)30246-5; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593617; lifespan.io (Cleara Biotech)

### SS-31 (Elamipretide, MTP-131, Bendavia)
- **Class / type:** Mitochondria-targeting tetrapeptide; cardiolipin-binding agent. Recently FDA-approved for one orphan indication.
- **Identity:** D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe-NH2 (Dmt = 2',6'-dimethyltyrosine); C32H49N9O5; ~639.8 Da; CAS 736992-21-5. SS-31 = elamipretide = MTP-131 = Bendavia.
- **Mechanism / target:** Concentrates in inner mitochondrial membrane, binds cardiolipin, stabilizes cristae, reduces cardiolipin peroxidation/ROS, supports ATP production and mitophagy. Structural stabilizer, not a scavenger.
- **Research use cases:** Primary mitochondrial disease (Barth syndrome, mitochondrial myopathy), heart failure/ischemia-reperfusion, dry AMD/geographic atrophy, Friedreich ataxia, aging.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- mitochondrial myopathy dose-response signals (Neurology); Barth (MMPOWER) supported 2025 FDA approval; AMD/HF programs mixed, some endpoints not met. Animal -- corrected mitophagy in Barth mouse (Sci Rep 2024). Strongest human evidence in this category.
- **Side effects:** Injection-site reactions (SC), headache, GI symptoms; generally tolerable at studied doses.
- **Warnings/limitations:** 2025 approval is narrow (Barth); other uses investigational and several large endpoints not met. Research use only outside labeling.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized (research) or formulated injectable (clinical); store cold/dark. Reconstitute sterile/bacteriostatic water; non-natural residues improve stability. Avoid freeze-thaw.
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved (Sept 2025, Barth syndrome); investigational elsewhere. Assume WADA relevance; verify.
- **Sources:** ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11816484; neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000005255; nature.com/articles/s41598-024-64368-y

### Pinealon (EDR, Glu-Asp-Arg)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic tripeptide bioregulator (Khavinson); investigational neuroprotective. Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** Glu-Asp-Arg (EDR); ~418-420 Da; no standardized CAS. Pineal-bioregulator lineage.
- **Mechanism / target:** Proposed nuclear/DNA-major-groove binding modulating neuronal-survival, antioxidant and neuroendocrine genes; reduces ROS, preserves mitochondrial potential, delayed ERK1/2 activation; claimed partial oral bioavailability.
- **Research use cases:** Neuroprotection (oxidative stress, hypoxia, ischemia), neuronal aging, cognition, Alzheimer's-relevant gene expression.
- **Reported benefits:** In vitro (2011) -- reduced ROS/necrosis, delayed ERK1/2 (PMC7795577). Animal -- neuroprotection, reduced apoptosis/lipid peroxidation. Human -- no robust independent data. Preliminary.
- **Side effects:** No characterized human AE profile; reported tolerable in limited preclinical work.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; preliminary single-lineage evidence. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store -20C dark/dry. Reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water. Refrigerate; very short half-life.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA/EMA approved; research chemical. Not WADA-named (verify).
- **Sources:** pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7795577; peptidedeck.com/peptides/pinealon

### Thymalin (Thymus polypeptide bioregulator)
- **Class / type:** Thymus-derived peptide bioregulator/immunomodulator (Khavinson tradition). Used clinically in Russia; not US/EU approved.
- **Identity:** Historically a low-MW polypeptide complex from calf thymus (a mixture, not one molecule). Some vendors equate it with dipeptide Glu-Trp (~333 Da) -- a labeling ambiguity to flag. Active short-peptide motifs include KE, EW, EDP.
- **Mechanism / target:** Proposed action via constituent short peptides binding DNA/histones to regulate immune genes; restores T-cell maturation, rebalances immune indices.
- **Research use cases:** Immunosenescence/immune restoration, T-cell deficiency, infection resistance, severe respiratory infection (incl. COVID-19).
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- Khavinson elderly cohorts: improved T-cell counts, CD4/CD8, NK activity; long follow-ups reduced infection/improved survival (PubMed 14523363); COVID-19 immune-status paper (Adv Gerontol 2021). Largely single-lineage, older; preliminary.
- **Side effects:** Reported well tolerated (IM in Russian protocols); no robust modern AE dataset; tissue-extract immunogenicity/variability concerns.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not FDA/EMA approved; extract composition/potency varies by source; caution in autoimmunity (immunostimulation). Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store frozen/refrigerated, dark/dry. Reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water; refrigerate; short-lived constituents.
- **Regulatory:** Used in Russia; not US/EU approved -- research chemical. Not WADA-named (verify).
- **Sources:** pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14523363; link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S2079057021040068

### Thymosin Alpha-1 (Talpha1, Thymalfasin, Zadaxin)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic 28-aa immunomodulatory peptide; approved drug in 30+ countries (not US-central). Defined molecule.
- **Identity:** Ac-SDAAVDTSSEITTKDLKEKKEVVEEAEN-OH; ~3108 Da; DrugBank DB04900. N-terminal fragment of prothymosin alpha. Brand Zadaxin.
- **Mechanism / target:** Immunomodulator recalibrating T-cell maturation and Th1/Th2 balance; TLR9->MyD88->IRF7 with IDO induction; enhances dendritic-cell/NK/T-cell function.
- **Research use cases:** Chronic hepatitis B/C, cancer immune adjunct, vaccine adjuvant, sepsis/severe infection (incl. COVID-19), immunosenescence.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- most validated here: >20 trials, thousands of patients; approved in 30+ countries for hepatitis B/C and cancer adjunct; improved viral response and immune parameters. Strong mechanistic support.
- **Side effects:** Generally well tolerated; injection-site reactions most common; serious AEs uncommon.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not US FDA/EMA central-approved (US off-label/investigational, subject to FDA compounding action). Caution in transplant immunosuppression and autoimmunity. Research use only in US.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized (Zadaxin powder); store refrigerated 2-8C, dark; do not freeze formulated product. Reconstitute supplied/sterile diluent; use promptly; N-acetyl group aids stability.
- **Regulatory:** Approved (thymalfasin) in 30+ countries; not US central-approved. Not WADA-named (verify; immunomodulator).
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB04900; fda.gov/media/183892/download

# 2. Metabolic Cofactors & Redox Compounds

### NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide, injectable)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous redox coenzyme; injectable use is compounded (non-FDA-approved).
- **Identity:** C21H27N7O14P2; ~663.43 g/mol; CAS 53-84-9. Redox pair NAD+/NADH; phosphorylated analog NADP+/NADPH.
- **Mechanism / target:** Electron carrier (glycolysis, TCA, oxidative phosphorylation) plus consumed co-substrate for sirtuins (SIRT1-7), PARPs, and CD38/SARM1. Extracellular NAD+ is poorly cell-permeable; much "NAD+ therapy" benefit is attributed to precursor delivery (nicotinamide, NMN, NR).
- **Research use cases:** Aging/senescence, mitochondrial/metabolic dysfunction, neurodegeneration models, addiction/withdrawal protocols (weak evidence), cardiometabolic/performance. Rigorous human trials mostly use oral precursors.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal/in vitro -- precursor repletion improves mitochondrial/sirtuin function. Human -- NR (NRPT) safely raised blood NAD+ (PMC5701244); NMN review showed modest performance gains (PMC11365583). Direct injectable NAD+ human efficacy sparse/uncontrolled; preliminary.
- **Side effects:** Rapid IV: chest tightness/pressure, flushing, nausea, cramping, lightheadedness (rate-dependent; mitigated by slow infusion). Injection-site reactions SC/IM. High-dose nicotinamide-family can elevate liver enzymes.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Compounded; not FDA-approved; PK of parenteral NAD+ poorly characterized. Caution hepatic disease at high dose; no pregnancy data.
- **Handling:** Hygroscopic, light/heat-sensitive lyophilizate; store cold/dark (often -20C). Reconstituted solution unstable -- use promptly; dilute IV and infuse slowly.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved (compounded injectable); oral precursors sold as supplements (NMN status contested). Not controlled; not WADA-prohibited.
- **Sources:** pubchem CID 5892; ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5701244; PMC11365583

### Glutathione (reduced, GSH)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous thiol tripeptide; principal intracellular antioxidant.
- **Identity:** gamma-L-Glu-L-Cys-Gly; C10H17N3O6S; ~307.32 g/mol; CAS 70-18-8. Cysteine thiol is the reactive group; oxidized form GSSG.
- **Mechanism / target:** Radical/electrophile scavenger; substrate for glutathione peroxidases and S-transferases (phase II detox); maintains protein thiols, regenerates vitamins C/E; redox state tracks GSH:GSSG.
- **Research use cases:** Oxidative stress, NAFLD, chemo-induced neuropathy (platinum co-protectant), Parkinson's, IV "detox"/wellness, (controversial) skin-lightening. NAC is the established route to raise GSH.
- **Reported benefits:** In vitro/animal -- robust cytoprotection/detox. Human -- IV GSH before cisplatin/oxaliplatin reduced neuropathy in some trials (mixed); NAFLD oxidative-marker/enzyme improvement in small studies (PMC10075255). Oral bioavailability low. Skin-lightening/wellness IV claims lack controlled evidence.
- **Side effects:** Generally tolerated short-term. IV may induce reductive stress (PubMed 40408806); cramping, bloating, hypersensitivity; IV skin-lightening linked to serious harm (SJS-type, renal/hepatic dysfunction, embolism/infection). Inhaled can trigger bronchospasm.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Short half-life, rebound after IV; unregulated IV/whitening flagged unsafe by FDA; asthma caution. Limited efficacy data outside chemoprotection.
- **Handling:** Thiol oxidizes readily (air/light/metals/heat) to GSSG -- store cold/dry/dark; reconstitute fresh; sulfurous odor as it oxidizes; incompatible with oxidizers.
- **Regulatory:** Oral supplement; injectable is compounded (not FDA-approved); FDA warns vs injectable skin-lightening. Not controlled; not WADA-prohibited.
- **Sources:** pubchem CID 124886; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10075255; PMC3162377

### Melatonin (N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous indoleamine neurohormone; chronobiotic/antioxidant; US OTC supplement.
- **Identity:** C13H16N2O2; ~232.28 g/mol; CAS 73-31-4.
- **Mechanism / target:** Agonist at MT1/MT2 GPCRs (Gi/Go) regulating circadian rhythm, sleep onset, SCN phase; receptor-independent radical scavenger and MT3/QR2 site; nocturnal secretion suppressed by light.
- **Research use cases:** Insomnia/sleep latency, circadian disorders (jet lag, shift work, delayed phase, non-24 in blind), pediatric/neurodevelopmental sleep, perioperative anxiety, antioxidant/neuroprotection, delirium prevention.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal/in vitro -- potent antioxidant/neuroprotectant. Human meta-analyses -- modest reduction in sleep-onset latency, reliable phase-shifting; small effect for primary insomnia. Oncology/neuroprotective human data preliminary.
- **Side effects:** Low toxicity; daytime drowsiness/grogginess, headache, dizziness, nausea, vivid dreams; mild hypotension, next-day sluggishness at high dose. US OTC products frequently mislabeled; rising pediatric ingestions.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Interacts with anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, antihypertensives, diabetes meds, CYP1A2 (fluvoxamine raises levels); sedative/alcohol caution; limited pregnancy data; wrong-time dosing shifts clock unintentionally.
- **Handling:** Light/oxidation-sensitive; store cool/dry/dark. Lipophilic (poorly water-soluble) -- injectables need co-solvents. Short half-life (~20-50 min IR).
- **Regulatory:** US OTC supplement; prescription medicine in EU/UK/AUS/Canada. Not controlled; not WADA-prohibited.
- **Sources:** pubchem CID 896; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3874706; nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28111-3

### Vitamin B12 (Methylcobalamin / Cyanocobalamin, injectable)
- **Class / type:** Water-soluble cobalt-corrinoid coenzyme; injectable B12 is FDA-approved for deficiency.
- **Identity:** Cyanocobalamin C63H88CoN14O14P (~1355.37; CAS 68-19-9); Methylcobalamin C63H91CoN13O14P (~1344.38; CAS 13422-55-4).
- **Mechanism / target:** Cofactor for methionine synthase (remethylates homocysteine -> methionine/SAMe; supports methylation, regenerates THF) and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Cyanocobalamin is a prodrug -> methyl-/adenosyl-cobalamin. Injection bypasses intrinsic-factor absorption (pernicious anemia).
- **Research use cases:** B12 deficiency (pernicious anemia, malabsorption, post-bariatric, vegan, metformin/PPI use), megaloblastic anemia, neuropathy/SCD, homocysteine lowering; methylcobalamin in diabetic neuropathy.
- **Reported benefits:** Human (established) -- corrects deficiency anemia/neurologic deficits; both forms restore status (NCT05785585). "Energy boost" in non-deficient people not supported.
- **Side effects:** Very safe; injection-site pain, mild diarrhea, itch/rash; rare anaphylaxis (cobalt/preservative); aggressive correction can cause hypokalemia/rebound thrombocytosis; cyanocobalamin's small cyanide moiety matters only in severe renal impairment or Leber/tobacco amblyopia.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Avoid cyanocobalamin in Leber optic neuropathy/tobacco amblyopia; cobalt hypersensitivity contraindication; caution severe renal impairment; folate masks hematologic but not neurologic deficiency.
- **Handling:** Intensely red/pink crystalline solids; markedly light-sensitive (methylcobalamin especially) -- protect from light, refrigerate where directed; compatible with saline.
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved Rx/OTC drug; also supplement. Not controlled; not WADA-prohibited.
- **Sources:** ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555964; NBK559132; clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05785585

### L-Carnitine (incl. Acetyl-L-Carnitine / ALCAR; levocarnitine)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous quaternary-amine amino-acid derivative; conditionally essential nutrient; levocarnitine is FDA-approved for deficiency.
- **Identity:** L-Carnitine C7H15NO3 (~161.20; CAS 541-15-1); ALCAR C9H17NO4 (~203.24; CAS 3040-38-8; HCl 5080-50-2).
- **Mechanism / target:** Carnitine shuttle (CPT1/CACT/CPT2) transports long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for beta-oxidation; buffers acyl-CoA/CoA. ALCAR crosses BBB, donates acetyl groups (acetylcholine/acetyl-CoA), neurotrophic/antioxidant.
- **Research use cases:** Primary/secondary carnitine deficiency, FAO disorders, dialysis depletion, CVD (angina/HF/post-MI), male infertility, exercise recovery; ALCAR for cognitive aging, diabetic/chemo neuropathy, depression; NAFLD.
- **Reported benefits:** Human (established) -- corrects deficiency. Supportive/mixed -- reduced liver fat/enzymes in NAFLD (PMC7469009); modest angina/post-MI/fertility signals; ALCAR meta-analysis reduced depressive symptoms (2018). Animal -- improved FAO/mitochondrial function.
- **Side effects:** ~3 g/day: nausea, vomiting, cramps, diarrhea, fishy odor (TMA); rare uremic weakness, seizures in seizure disorders. TMAO/CVD association observational (Mendelian-randomization generally does not support causation).
- **Warnings/limitations:** Use only L (levo) form -- D/DL are toxic (myasthenia-like weakness). Caution seizure history, hypothyroidism, uremia/HD, warfarin (INR). Limited pregnancy data.
- **Handling:** Highly hygroscopic -- store dry/sealed/cool; water-soluble, stable near neutral pH; injectable levocarnitine is sterile aqueous.
- **Regulatory:** Levocarnitine FDA-approved Rx (oral/IV); L-carnitine/ALCAR also supplements. Not controlled; WADA-permitted (only IV-infusion volume limits apply).
- **Sources:** lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/L-carnitine; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7469009

### AICAR (Acadesine; AICA-riboside)
- **Class / type:** Cell-permeable purine nucleoside analog; pharmacologic AMPK activator ("exercise mimetic"). Investigational; WADA-prohibited.
- **Identity:** C9H14N4O5; ~258.23 g/mol; CAS 2627-69-2. Riboside of the purine intermediate ZMP.
- **Mechanism / target:** Phosphorylated to ZMP (AMP mimetic), allosterically activates AMPK gamma subunit and protects Thr172; increases muscle glucose uptake and fatty-acid oxidation, suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis/lipogenesis, inhibits mTORC1. ZMP also perturbs other AMP-sensitive enzymes.
- **Research use cases:** AMPK/metabolic-stress tool; T2D/insulin resistance/obesity models; endurance research; cardioprotection (acadesine CABG trials); diabetic polyneuropathy (mitophagy); oncology; CLL.
- **Reported benefits:** Predominantly in vitro/animal -- glucose uptake, FAO, insulin sensitivity, increased running endurance (Narkar et al.). Human -- limited; acadesine IV cardioprotection in CABG (reduced some events), never approved. No established human metabolic/performance efficacy.
- **Side effects:** Acadesine relatively well tolerated in cardiac trials; transient hyperuricemia, headache, infusion effects. AMPK activation context-dependently tumor-promoting; long-term healthy-human safety unknown.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; no safe non-clinical dose; WADA-prohibited; hyperuricemia/gout caution; mostly preclinical.
- **Handling:** White crystalline solid; store cold/desiccated/dark; soluble in water/DMSO; use solutions fresh.
- **Regulatory:** Investigational/research chemical; not DEA-controlled. WADA-PROHIBITED (S4.5 AMPK activators, all times, since 2011).
- **Sources:** selleckchem.com/products/aicar; pubchem CID 17513; usada.org (AICAR advisory)

### 5-Amino-1MQ (5-amino-1-methylquinolinium)
- **Class / type:** Small molecule (NOT a peptide despite marketing); NNMT inhibitor; investigational research chemical.
- **Identity:** Cation C10H11N2+ (~159.21); iodide salt C10H11IN2 (~286.11); CAS commonly cited 5611-99-4 (verify per CoA). Mimics 1-methylnicotinamide.
- **Mechanism / target:** Selective NNMT inhibitor. NNMT (high in adipose/liver) methylates nicotinamide using SAM; overactivity drains NAD+ salvage and SAM methyl flux. Inhibition preserves nicotinamide for NAD+ and restores methylation, increasing adipocyte energy expenditure. "Block consumption" rather than "add precursor."
- **Research use cases:** Obesity, metabolic syndrome/insulin resistance, fat-loss/energy-expenditure, NAD+/SAM metabolism; marketed for weight management.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal -- diet-induced-obese mice: reduced weight/white-fat mass without changed intake, lower cholesterol, smaller adipocytes. In vitro -- increased adipocyte NAD+/expenditure. Human -- NO completed published trials; all claims animal/in-vitro; preliminary.
- **Side effects:** No AEs in short rodent studies; essentially no human safety data. Theoretical: broad NNMT/1-MNA roles, SAM-methylation perturbation, unknown chronic NAD+/methyl shifts; toxicity/carcinogenicity/reproductive safety uncharacterized.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Strictly investigational; no human approval; animal/in-vitro only, short exposures; no defined human dose/PK. Verify salt/CAS per CoA.
- **Handling:** Solid (iodide salt); store cold/desiccated/dark (light-sensitive). Water/DMSO-soluble; prepare fresh.
- **Regulatory:** Not approved; research chemical. Not WADA-named (NAD/metabolic-modulator scrutiny -- caution).
- **Sources:** rawamino.com (NNMT/5-amino-1MQ); newtropin.com; pubchem (identity confirmation)

# 3. Healing, Repair & Copper Peptides

### BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound-157, PL 14736)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic pentadecapeptide ("cytoprotective"). Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** GEPPPGKPADDAGLV (15 aa); ~1419 Da; C62H98N16O22; CAS 137525-51-0. Acetate salt common; stabilized arginine-salt used in most rodent studies.
- **Mechanism / target:** No confirmed receptor. Proposed (Sikiric group, rodent): Src-Caveolin-1-eNOS/nitric-oxide upregulation; angiogenesis (VEGFR2); dopaminergic/serotonergic/GABAergic modulation; GH-receptor expression in tendon fibroblasts; EGR-1/NAB2/collagen. Preliminary, single-group.
- **Research use cases:** Tendon/ligament/muscle/bone healing; GI mucosal protection/ulcer/colitis/fistula; IBD (PL 14736 trialed); liver injury; wound healing; neuroprotection; thrombosis. Overwhelmingly preclinical.
- **Reported benefits:** Rodent in-vivo and in-vitro -- accelerated healing, angiogenesis, NSAID-GI protection. Human -- very limited; only PL 14736 early IBD/periodontal trials. Injectable musculoskeletal claims unestablished in humans.
- **Side effects:** Rodent tox benign (not adequate human data); human AE data essentially absent. FDA flags immunogenicity, unknown long-term effects, pro-angiogenic theoretical tumor-vascularization risk; product-quality/impurity risk in unregulated supply.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; no established human dose/route/safety; pro-angiogenic malignancy caveat; single-group preclinical base. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store cold (2-8C; -20C long-term) dark/dry. Reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water (swirl, don't shake); solution less stable -- refrigerate, use within days-weeks. Stable in gastric juice (oral routes appear in literature); arginate salt aids stability.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; FDA 503A Category 2 (significant safety risk) -- restricted in compounding. Research chemical. WADA S0 catch-all applies.
- **Sources:** ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8275860; PMC7555539; fda.gov (503A bulk substances)

### TB-500 / Thymosin Beta-4 (Tbeta4)
- **Class / type:** Two related entities under overlapping names. Tbeta4 = natural 43-residue G-actin-sequestering peptide; "TB-500" = marketed synthetic fragment (often acetylated heptapeptide Ac-LKKTETQ, ~17-23). Many products are mislabeled/full-length -- a key ambiguity.
- **Identity:** Tbeta4 ~4921 Da, CAS 77591-33-4; TB-500 fragment ~889 Da. Fragment retains LKKTET motif but lacks the parent's anti-fibrotic Ac-SDKP region.
- **Mechanism / target:** Sequesters monomeric G-actin, regulating polymerization/cell migration/proliferation; Tbeta4 also promotes angiogenesis, survival signaling, reduces inflammation/fibrosis, recruits stem/progenitor cells. Fragment presumed to act mainly via actin motif.
- **Research use cases:** Dermal/corneal wound healing; cardiac repair post-MI (Tbeta4 reached clinical investigation); tendon/ligament/muscle injury; hair growth; CNS injury. Equine/veterinary "TB-500" use is off-label.
- **Reported benefits:** In-vitro/animal -- wound closure, angiogenesis, reduced scarring, cardioprotection. Human -- only full-length Tbeta4 early-phase (dermal/corneal/cardiac), mixed/modest; does NOT validate injectable "TB-500" fragment products.
- **Side effects:** Limited human safety data; early Tbeta4 trials tolerable. Pro-angiogenic theoretical tumor risk; immunogenicity; fragment-vs-full-length mislabeling.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; malignancy caveat; identity ambiguity. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store cold/dark, frozen long-term; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, swirl; hydrophilic, fairly stable refrigerated short-term. Verify identity (labeling often mismatches).
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved. WADA-PROHIBITED since 2011 (S2), all times. Flagged in FDA compounding review.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymosin_beta-4; bscg.org (TB-500 ban status); fda.gov (503A)

### KPV (Lysine-Proline-Valine; alpha-MSH 11-13)

### GHK-Cu (Copper Tripeptide-1)
- **Class / type:** Natural copper(II)-binding tripeptide; matrikine/signaling peptide from plasma. Widely used cosmetic active; studied as regenerative/wound agent.
- **Identity:** Gly-His-Lys complexed with Cu(II). Free GHK ~340.4 Da, CAS 49557-75-7; GHK-Cu complex CAS 89030-95-5 (also 130120-57-9). Plasma levels decline with age (~200 ng/mL at 20 to ~80 at 60).

Human -- controlled cosmetic-dermatology studies: improved firmness/elasticity, reduced fine lines/photodamage (topical), diabetic-ulcer/post-Mohs healing. ### Combination Stacks (Healing/Repair)
No controlled human combination-trial evidence exists; combining compounds compounds the individual identity/quality/safety caveats.

- **BPC-157 + TB-500:** Most common pairing; cited "recruitment + remodeling" rationale (TB-500 mobilizes repair cells via actin; BPC-157 supports local vascular/matrix repair and GI cytoprotection). Both pro-angiogenic (double malignancy caveat); TB-500 WADA-prohibited and BPC-157 under WADA S0 (unusable in tested sport); TB-500 identity ambiguity persists. Preclinical.
- **GLOW (TB-500 + BPC-157 + GHK-Cu):** Adds GHK-Cu for skin/cosmetic and broad regenerative signaling. Introduces copper (load/stability), three pro-angiogenic agents amplify theoretical malignancy caveat; GHK-Cu human evidence is topical, not injectable-blend validated.
- **KLOW (TB-500 + BPC-157 + GHK-Cu + KPV):** GLOW plus KPV's anti-inflammatory axis (NF-kB/cytokine/PepT1). Stacks four unapproved peptides with four impurity/identity profiles; KPV evidence is gut/colitis cell+rodent, not a systemic cocktail; copper and triple-angiogenic caveats carry over; TB-500 prohibited, rest unapproved. No combination human data.

### Cerebrolysin (FPF-1070)
- **Class / type:** Peptidergic neurotrophic preparation -- porcine brain enzymatic hydrolysate (mixture, not one peptide). Approved abroad, not US.
- **Identity:** ~25% defined peptides (<10 kDa) plus free amino acids; fragments of NGF/enkephalins/orexin/galanin. No single CAS.
- **Mechanism / target:** Mimics/modulates neurotrophic factors (BDNF/NGF/GDNF/CNTF); PI3K/Akt; upregulates Bcl-2, reduces caspase-3; reduces excitotoxicity; modulates amyloid processing/plasticity.

Mixed: large CASTA trial negative on primary endpoint; 2020 Cochrane found insufficient evidence in acute ischemic stroke. Animal -- consistent neuroprotection.

### DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide)

### Selank (TP-7)

### VIP / Aviptadil (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide; RLF-100)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous 28-aa neuropeptide hormone (secretin/glucagon superfamily); aviptadil is the synthetic pharma form. Investigational (orphan designations).

### ARA-290 (Cibinetide)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic 11-aa non-hematopoietic EPO-derived tissue-protective peptide; investigational (orphan designation).

# 5. Growth-Hormone Axis -- GHRH Analogs & Secretagogues

> All compounds in this section are WADA-PROHIBITED (S2) as GH secretagogues / GH-releasing factors, at all times. General GH-axis concerns (glucose tolerance, theoretical promotion of occult neoplasia) apply across the class.

### CJC-1295 With DAC (DAC:GRF)
- **Class / type:** Long-acting synthetic GHRH analog (GHRH-R agonist). Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** Tetrasubstituted GRF(1-29) (D-Ala2, Gln8, Ala15, Leu27) + maleimidopropionyl-lysine DAC linker binding albumin; ~3647 Da (peptide ~3368 Da); CAS family 863288-34-0.
- **Mechanism / target:** GHRH-R agonist; DAC covalently binds albumin (reactive maleimide), resisting DPP-IV/renal clearance -> ~6-8 day half-life and sustained ("bleed") GH/IGF-1 rather than pulses.
- **Research use cases:** Long-acting GH secretagogue; historically explored for GH deficiency; GH/IGF-1 axis research. Original ConjuChem development discontinued.
- **Reported benefits:** Human (Teichman 2006, JCEM) -- single SC dose raised GH 2-10x for ~6 days, IGF-1 1.5-3x for 9-11 days, sustained with weekly/biweekly dosing. Animal -- normalized growth in GHRH-KO mice. Limited beyond early trials.
- **Side effects:** Generally tolerated in Teichman trial; injection-site reactions, facial flushing, headache, water retention. Sustained non-pulsatile GH -> theoretical receptor desensitization; ConjuChem cardiac program contributed to halting development.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; no chronic-use safety; sustained supraphysiologic GH/IGF-1 risks (edema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel, insulin resistance, neoplasia caveat). Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store cold/dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate, use within weeks; reactive DAC maleimide moisture-sensitive; avoid freeze-thaw. Albumin-binding PK differs fundamentally from no-DAC.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; research chemical. WADA S2.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJC-1295; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683

### CJC-1295 Without DAC (Mod GRF 1-29)
- **Class / type:** Short-acting synthetic GHRH analog. Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** Tetrasubstituted GRF(1-29) amide (D-Ala2, Gln8, Ala15, Leu27); C152H252N44O42; ~3368 Da. No DAC.
- **Mechanism / target:** GHRH-R agonist evoking a discrete GH pulse; substitutions reduce DPP-IV cleavage/raise potency, but ~30-min half-life keeps release pulsatile (closer to physiology).
- **Research use cases:** Pulsatile GHRH stimulus; frequently paired with a GHRP to study synergy. Terminology largely from research-chemical literature.
- **Reported benefits:** Mechanistic plausibility from native GHRH/GRF(1-29) physiology and GHRH+GHRP synergy; direct controlled trials of this specific analog sparse; preliminary.
- **Side effects:** Anecdotal/class -- injection-site reactions, flushing, headache, lightheadedness, mild water retention; short half-life limits exposure; robust human safety data lacking.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; GH-axis concerns apply. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store cold/dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate, minimize freeze-thaw. Short half-life = timing-sensitive; often co-administered with a GHRP. Frequently mislabeled "CJC-1295" without DAC distinction.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; research chemical. WADA S2.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJC-1295; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683

### Sermorelin (GRF 1-29; formerly Geref)
- **Class / type:** GHRH analog -- unmodified bioactive 1-29 fragment of human GHRH. Was FDA-approved; now compounded.
- **Identity:** GHRH(1-29) amide; C149H246N44O42S; ~3358 Da; CAS 86168-78-7 (acetate 114466-38-5).
- **Mechanism / target:** GHRH-R agonist evoking physiologic pulsatile GH (needs intact pituitary); short half-life (~10-20 min, DPP-IV) preserves pulsatility/feedback.
- **Research use cases:** Diagnostic for pituitary GH reserve; was approved for pediatric GH deficiency; adult GH-axis/aging research; GHRH reference standard.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- as Geref, increased growth velocity in GHD children, reliable GH rise in stimulation testing; adult/aging studies showed modest IGF-1/GH-pulse increases. Strong human evidence (product discontinued for commercial, not safety, reasons).
- **Side effects:** Injection-site reactions, flushing, headache, dizziness, nausea, rare hypersensitivity; antibody formation with chronic pediatric use.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Needs functional pituitary; contraindicated in hypersensitivity; response blunted by obesity/hyperglycemia/somatostatin tone/glucocorticoids/thyroid issues.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized acetate; store cold/dark; reconstitute sterile diluent; short-lived solution -- refrigerate, use promptly. DPP-IV sensitivity drove CJC/Mod-GRF substitutions. Compounded via 503A/503B in US.
- **Regulatory:** Was FDA-approved (Geref), withdrawn (not safety); now compounded Rx. WADA S2.
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00010; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermorelin

### Tesamorelin (Egrifta, Egrifta SV/WR; TH9507)
- **Class / type:** GHRH analog -- stabilized synthetic hGHRH(1-44). The only FDA-approved compound in this section.
- **Identity:** hGHRH(1-44) with trans-3-hexenoyl on N-terminal Tyr (DPP-IV resistance); C221H366N72O67S; ~5135.9 Da; CAS 218949-48-5 (acetate 901758-09-6).
- **Mechanism / target:** GHRH-R agonist; pulsatile GH/IGF-1 with preserved feedback; reduces visceral adipose via GH/IGF-1 lipolytic axis.
- **Research use cases:** FDA-approved for HIV-associated lipodystrophy visceral-fat reduction; researched for NAFLD/MASLD hepatic fat (HIV) and cognition (MCI).
- **Reported benefits:** Strong human RCTs (Falutz NEJM 2007; Stanley) -- ~15-18% VAT reduction over 26 wk vs placebo, reduced triglycerides; reduced hepatic fat (JAMA 2014); small NIH RCT suggested cognitive benefit. Best human evidence in this section.
- **Side effects:** Arthralgia, injection-site reactions, peripheral edema, myalgia, paresthesia, reduced glucose tolerance/elevated IGF-1, hypersensitivity/rash; glucose intolerance/new-onset diabetes labeled.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Contraindicated in active malignancy, pituitary disorders, pregnancy, hypersensitivity to tesamorelin/mannitol; monitor IGF-1/glucose; not for general weight loss; benefit reverses on discontinuation.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized + sterile water; daily SC. F4 stored room-temp; newer F8 (Egrifta WR/SV) weekly reconstitution, reduced volume -- follow formulation-specific storage. Improper reconstitution causes aggregation.
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved (Egrifta 2010; SV; WR). WADA S2 (therapeutic use needs TUE).
- **Sources:** accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/022505s020lbl.pdf; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18046028; 25027139

### Hexarelin (Examorelin, EP-23905)
- **Class / type:** GHRP / ghrelin mimetic (GHS-R1a agonist); methylated GHRP-6 super-analog. Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2; C47H58N12O6; ~887.04 Da; CAS 140703-51-1.
- **Mechanism / target:** Potent GHS-R1a agonist (2-methyl-Trp raises potency/stability); pulsatile GH, GHRH synergy; binds cardiac/vascular CD36 (cardiovascular interest); stimulates ACTH/cortisol/prolactin like GHRP-2.
- **Research use cases:** Potent GH secretagogue; distinctively cardiovascular (CD36 cardioprotection) in animal/small human studies.
- **Reported benefits:** Human (Ghigo/Imbimbo) -- potent GH release, GHRH synergy; cardiovascular animal/small human signals via GH-independent CD36. Notable: chronic dosing causes GH-response attenuation (desensitization/tachyphylaxis).
- **Side effects:** ACTH/cortisol/prolactin elevation (like GHRP-2); GH desensitization with continued use; flushing, headache, injection-site reactions, water retention.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; tachyphylaxis limits sustained efficacy; cortisol/prolactin and GH-axis concerns. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store cold/dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate, minimize freeze-thaw. Most potent classic hexapeptide GHRP; most prone to desensitization.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved (clinically investigated, never marketed); research chemical. WADA S2.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexarelin; go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB12086

### Ipamorelin (NNC 26-0161)
- **Class / type:** Selective GHRP / ghrelin mimetic (selective GHS-R1a agonist). Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2 (Aib, D-2-naphthylalanine); C38H49N9O5; ~711.85 Da; CAS 170851-70-4.
- **Mechanism / target:** Selective GHS-R1a agonist; pulsatile GH, GHRH synergy; at GH-releasing doses does NOT meaningfully raise ACTH/cortisol/prolactin/appetite (Novo Nordisk "clean" secretagogue).
- **Research use cases:** Selective GH secretagogue / ghrelin-receptor tool; investigated (Novo Phase 2) for postoperative ileus/GI motility before discontinuation.
- **Reported benefits:** Preclinical (Raun 1998) -- GH release with no ACTH/cortisol/prolactin rise (basis of "selective" reputation). Human GI-motility program run, discontinued (not a safety failure). GH-axis human benefit plausible by class, not established in large trials.
- **Side effects:** Best-tolerated GHRP (spares cortisol/prolactin/appetite); anecdotal/class -- injection-site reactions, flushing, headache, lightheadedness, mild water retention; limited human data.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; GH-axis concerns with sustained use. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store cold/dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate, avoid freeze-thaw. Selectivity makes it the GHRP most often paired with a GHRH analog.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved (was Novo clinical candidate); research chemical. WADA S2.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipamorelin; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9849822

### Combination: CJC-1295 (No-DAC) + Ipamorelin
Cited as a dual-pathway GH-stimulation model recruiting two distinct somatotroph axes: CJC-1295 no-DAC (GHRH-R agonist) drives GH synthesis/release while ipamorelin (GHS-R1a agonist) activates the ghrelin receptor AND suppresses hypothalamic somatostatin tone, removing the brake; the two pathways amplify each other -- the GHRH+GHRP synergy documented in human studies (Bowers, Ghigo). No-DAC is chosen for a discrete pulsatile release mimicking physiology (vs the DAC "bleed"); ipamorelin is the preferred partner for GHS-R1a selectivity (avoids cortisol/prolactin/appetite). Synergy is well-supported by human GHRH+GHRP physiology, but controlled efficacy/safety data for this specific research-chemical pairing are limited/preliminary. Both components research-use-only and WADA-prohibited.

# 6. GH / IGF / Anabolic Compounds

> All five are WADA-banned (S2/S4) at all times. Only somatropin is an approved medicine.



### HGH Fragment 176-191 (Frag 176-191)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic 16-residue C-terminal GH fragment (lipolytic class); related to AOD9604. Research-chemical only.
- **Identity:** GH residues 176-191 (disulfide-bridged loop); ~1817 g/mol; CAS 221231-10-3; PubChem CID 172966176 (vendor specs vary -- preliminary).
- **Mechanism / target:** Reproduces GH's lipolytic C-terminal activity without GH growth/IGF-1/diabetogenic actions; stimulates lipolysis (HSL), inhibits lipogenesis, increases ADRB3 expression. Lipolysis is NOT directly beta-3-AR mediated (Heffernan, Endocrinology 2001) -- receptor is permissive/required downstream.
- **Research use cases:** Adipose lipid metabolism/fat oxidation/body-weight (obesity models), ADRB3 regulation, exploratory cartilage/OA.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal -- obese (ob/ob) mice: reduced weight, increased fat oxidation, restored ADRB3 RNA. In-vitro -- increased adipocyte lipolysis. Human -- minimal for the fragment itself; most human evidence via AOD9604 (modest, ultimately non-significant). Weak for human fat-loss claims.
- **Side effects:** Limited formal human safety data; lacks GH diabetogenic/IGF-1 effects, so GH-type glucose effects not expected at studied levels; mainly injection-site reactions in unregulated use; long-term safety uncharacterized.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; no human dosing; human efficacy unproven; grey-market identity/purity a major limitation. Research use only.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store -20C dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic water slowly down wall (no foaming); refrigerate 2-8C, ~4 weeks; no freeze-thaw; cysteine/disulfide loop oxidation-sensitive.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; research use only. WADA-banned (S2/S0). Often conflated with AOD9604.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HGH_Fragment_176-191; academic.oup.com/endo/article/142/12/5182/2988749

### AOD9604 (Tyr-hGH 177-191)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic hexadecapeptide; modified C-terminal GH fragment; investigational anti-obesity (development discontinued).
- **Identity:** GH 176-191 with N-terminal Tyr substitution; 16 aa; developed by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals.
- **Mechanism / target:** Lipolytic/anti-lipogenic GH-fragment mimicry without IGF-1/insulin-antagonist effects; increases ADRB3; lipolysis not directly beta-3-AR mediated (receptor permissive).
- **Research use cases:** Obesity/fat metabolism, lipolysis/fat oxidation; explored in cartilage/OA and as nutraceutical.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal -- consistent weight reduction/increased fat oxidation in obese mice. Human -- early ~300-subject 12-wk trial suggested ~2.6-2.8 kg loss (1 mg) vs ~0.8 kg placebo, BUT the pivotal Phase 2b FAILED its primary endpoint; development discontinued, no Phase 3. Net human weight-loss efficacy negative/unproven.
- **Side effects:** Generally well tolerated in human trials at studied oral doses; no significant effect on glucose/IGF-1/GH axis short-term; long-term/injectable safety uncharacterized.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Failed efficacy is the headline; not an approved medicine; self-affirmed GRAS is not FDA efficacy approval.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store -20C dark; reconstitute gently with bacteriostatic water (no foam); refrigerate 2-8C, use within weeks, no freeze-thaw. Was also an oral tablet in the clinical program.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved (development halted); self-affirmed GRAS panel determination (not FDA drug approval); TGA Rx/unapproved in Australia. WADA-banned (S0/S2).
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOD9604; jofem.org (safety/tolerability)

### IGF-1 LR3 (Long R3 IGF-1)
- **Class / type:** Recombinant IGF-1 analog; modified growth-factor peptide; originally a cell-culture reagent. Research-only.
- **Identity:** 83 aa (native IGF-1 is 70): Arg substituted for Glu at position 3 + 13-residue N-terminal extension (MFPAMPLLSLFVN); ~9.1 kDa.
- **Mechanism / target:** IGF-1R (RTK) agonist via IRS-1/PI3K-Akt and MAPK (proliferation, protein synthesis, survival). Modifications cut IGFBP affinity ~600-fold -> circulates free, ~3x potency, much longer functional half-life (~20-30 h).
- **Research use cases:** Cell-culture growth factor (recombinant protein yield, proliferation/viability); IGF-1R signaling, muscle/tissue hypertrophy, IGFBP-independent IGF action.
- **Reported benefits:** In-vitro -- potent sustained mitogenic/pro-survival effects (its validated intended use). Animal -- tissue/muscle growth. Human -- no controlled efficacy data; anabolic/performance claims anecdotal/unproven. Preliminary beyond cell culture.
- **Side effects:** Mechanistic -- hypoglycemia (insulin-like, amplified by long half-life/IGFBP evasion), organ/tissue overgrowth, potential to promote existing neoplasia, acromegaly-like changes, fluid retention; non-clinical human safety uncharacterized.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not a human therapeutic; no human dosing; long half-life/IGFBP evasion amplify hypoglycemia/proliferative risk; cancer-promotion caveat. Laboratory reagent.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store -20C dark; reconstitute in dilute acetic acid or sterile/bacteriostatic water (per supplier); handle gently; aliquot/freeze for longer; avoid freeze-thaw.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved for humans (distinct from approved mecasermin/Increlex = native IGF-1); research/cell-culture reagent. WADA S2 (growth factors/mimetics).
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGF-1_LR3; prospecbio.com/long_r3_igf1_human

### Follistatin (FST, Follistatin-344)
- **Class / type:** Secreted regulatory glycoprotein; activin-binding/TGF-beta-superfamily antagonist (344-residue isoform). Research/experimental.
- **Identity:** 344-aa glycoprotein (FST-344; precursor to FST-315/FST-288); multi-domain (N-terminal + three FS domains). Parent: human FST gene.
- **Mechanism / target:** High-affinity neutralization of myostatin (GDF-8), activin A, GDF-11, several BMPs; prevents ActRIIA/ActRIIB engagement, blocking SMAD2/3 that suppresses muscle protein synthesis -> de-represses muscle growth.
- **Research use cases:** Myostatin/activin/SMAD research, skeletal-muscle hypertrophy/muscular dystrophy, reproductive/FSH biology, fibrosis/BMP, hair-follicle.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal -- AAV follistatin gene transfer gives large, durable muscle mass/strength gains (Haidet, PNAS 2008); FST-344 associated with greatest effect/good tolerability. Human -- minimal; localized AAV-follistatin Phase I/II (Becker/IBM) showed some functional improvement, acceptable short-term safety under strict localized expression. Systemic recombinant FST-344 human efficacy essentially unstudied; muscle-growth claims unproven.
- **Side effects:** Sparse human data; theoretical reproductive (FSH/activin), cardiac/vascular, tissue, and tumor-microenvironment effects from broad systemic myostatin/activin/BMP blockade. Animal gene-therapy reported no untoward cardiac/reproductive pathology at studied doses (not human systemic safety).
- **Warnings/limitations:** No approved human use/dosing; recombinant peptide PK poor (rapid clearance) -- durable effects come from gene delivery, a key limitation for injectable-peptide claims; broad ligand promiscuity extends effects beyond muscle.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store -20C (or colder) dark; reconstitute gently sterile/bacteriostatic water (avoid foam/shear); refrigerate short-term, aliquot/freeze longer; avoid freeze-thaw. Injected recombinant rapidly cleared.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; research/experimental (gene therapy investigational). WADA S4.5 (myostatin inhibitors), all times.
- **Sources:** pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0709144105; newtropin.com/peptides/follistatin-344

# 7. Reproductive, Sexual Health, Melanocortin & Cosmetic

### HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin; Pregnyl, Novarel, Ovidrel)
- **Class / type:** Glycoprotein gonadotropin hormone (placental); urinary or recombinant. FDA/EMA-approved.
- **Identity:** Heterodimer (alpha 92 aa shared; beta 145 aa unique), heavily glycosylated; ~36-40 kDa; CAS 9002-61-3 (choriogonadotropin alfa 56832-30-5).
- **Mechanism / target:** LH/CG receptor (LHCGR, Gs) agonist mimicking LH: females -- final follicular maturation/ovulation, corpus luteum progesterone; males -- Leydig testosterone/intratesticular testosterone supporting spermatogenesis. Long half-life (sialylated beta C-terminal peptide).
- **Research use cases:** Ovulation induction/ART trigger, male hypogonadotropic hypogonadism/fertility preservation, cryptorchidism; (discredited) weight loss.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- ovulation triggering/luteal support, restored testosterone/spermatogenesis, testicular descent. "hCG diet" not supported beyond calorie restriction.
- **Side effects:** OHSS (can be severe), multiple gestation, injection-site reactions, headache, fatigue, gynecomastia/edema (males), thromboembolism in OHSS.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Contraindicated in hormone-dependent tumors, precocious puberty, undiagnosed uterine bleeding; caution thrombophilia/asthma/cardiac/renal; interferes with hCG assays.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized + diluent; reconstitute gently (no vigorous shaking); lyophilate refrigerated (some room-temp); reconstituted refrigerated, used within label window (~30-60 d); recombinant Ovidrel is a refrigerated prefilled syringe.
- **Regulatory:** FDA/EMA-approved Rx. WADA: prohibited in males (S2; raises endogenous testosterone); not prohibited in females.
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB14750; ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532950

### HMG (Human Menopausal Gonadotropin / Menotropin; Menopur, Repronex)
- **Class / type:** Purified urinary gonadotropin (FSH + LH activity ~1:1; hCG contributes LH activity in HP-hMG). FDA/EMA-approved.
- **Identity:** Mixture of glycoprotein hormones (FSH ~30 kDa, LH ~30 kDa, plus hCG); not one molecule; CAS 9002-68-0.
- **Mechanism / target:** FSH at FSHR (granulosa: follicle growth/aromatase/estradiol) + LH/hCG at LHCGR (theca: androgen substrate/maturation); recruits/matures follicles; males -- spermatogenesis (with hCG).
- **Research use cases:** Controlled ovarian stimulation/IVF, ovulation induction in anovulatory infertility, spermatogenesis induction in hypogonadotropic men (with hCG).
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- established follicular recruitment/ovulation induction; meta-analyses show HP-hMG comparable live-birth to recombinant FSH with formulation differences.
- **Side effects:** OHSS, multiple pregnancy, injection-site reactions, abdominal pain/bloating, headache, rare thromboembolism (often OHSS-linked).
- **Warnings/limitations:** Contraindicated in primary ovarian/testicular failure, uncontrolled thyroid/adrenal dysfunction, hormone-dependent tumors, undiagnosed bleeding, non-PCOS ovarian cysts; needs ultrasound/estradiol monitoring for OHSS.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized + diluent; reconstitute by gentle swirling (no foam), use promptly; store per brand (refrigerated or controlled room temp); HP forms allow SC dosing.
- **Regulatory:** FDA/EMA-approved Rx. WADA: gonadotropins prohibited in males (S2).
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00032; ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534831

### Kisspeptin-10 (Kp-10, metastin 45-54)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous neuropeptide fragment (RFamide family); KISS1-derived decapeptide. Investigational.
- **Identity:** YNWNSFGLRF-NH2; ~C63H83N17O14; ~1302.5 Da; CAS 374675-21-5. Conserved C-terminal Arg-Phe-NH2 essential.
- **Mechanism / target:** Agonist at KISS1R/GPR54 (Gq/11) on hypothalamic GnRH neurons -> GnRH release -> pituitary LH (and FSH); upstream HPG-axis gatekeeper/puberty switch. Shorter half-life than kisspeptin-54.
- **Research use cases:** HPG-axis probing/stimulation, IVF ovulation/egg-maturation triggering (much strong data uses kisspeptin-54), diagnostic GnRH-neuron testing, hypothalamic amenorrhea/fertility, sexual/emotional brain processing; original metastasis-suppressor (oncology).
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- raises LH across cycle phases; can trigger oocyte maturation in IVF with low OHSS (mostly kisspeptin-54); fMRI sexual/emotional modulation. Animal/in-vitro -- GnRH-stimulating mechanism/puberty. Non-reproductive "benefits" preliminary.
- **Side effects:** Generally well tolerated in research infusions; mild (transient headache, injection-site); KISS1R tachyphylaxis with sustained exposure; limited long-term data.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Investigational; no approved product; HPG-axis effects inappropriate outside controlled study; short half-life/desensitization; reproductive safety not established.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store desiccated/frozen (-20C); reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water; Met/Trp oxidation-prone -- avoid freeze-thaw, protect from light.
- **Regulatory:** Not approved; investigational/reagent. HPG-modulators draw WADA scrutiny; no approved sport use.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisspeptin; ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6286967

### Oxytocin (Pitocin, Syntocinon)
- **Class / type:** Endogenous cyclic nonapeptide hormone/neuropeptide (posterior pituitary). FDA/EMA-approved.
- **Identity:** Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-NH2, disulfide Cys1-Cys6; ~1007.2 Da; CAS 50-56-6.
- **Mechanism / target:** Oxytocin receptor (OXTR, Gq) agonist; peripheral uterine and mammary myoepithelial contraction (parturition/let-down); central social bonding/trust/anxiety/stress; mild vasopressin cross-reactivity (antidiuresis at high dose).
- **Research use cases:** Obstetric (labor induction/augmentation, postpartum hemorrhage, milk let-down); neuroscience/psychiatry (intranasal for autism social deficits, anxiety, PTSD, addiction -- investigational).
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- established uterotonic/milk-ejection (approved). Animal -- robust pro-social/pair-bonding. Intranasal human autism/social-cognition trials mixed, often fail to replicate; psychiatric benefit unproven.
- **Side effects:** Obstetric IV -- uterine hyperstimulation/tetany, fetal distress, uterine rupture (misuse), water intoxication/hyponatremia (antidiuretic + hypotonic fluids), hypotension/tachycardia (rapid bolus); intranasal mild (nasal irritation, headache).
- **Warnings/limitations:** Obstetric contraindications (cephalopelvic disproportion, unfavorable positions, fetal distress, rupture-prone uterus); water-intoxication risk; intranasal psychiatric use unapproved (dosing/CNS penetration debated).
- **Handling:** Injectable solution typically refrigerated 2-8C; heat/oxidation-sensitive; disulfide reduction-sensitive; avoid freezing finished solution per labels. Research lyophilizate frozen/desiccated, reconstitute sterile water, dark, no freeze-thaw.
- **Regulatory:** FDA/EMA-approved Rx (injectable). Not WADA-prohibited. Intranasal psychiatric use investigational.
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB00107; ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507848

### PT-141 (Bremelanotide; Vyleesi)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic cyclic heptapeptide; melanocortin agonist (alpha-MSH analog); metabolite of melanotan II. FDA-approved.
- **Identity:** Ac-Nle-cyclo(Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys)-OH; ~1025.2 Da; CAS 189691-06-3.
- **Mechanism / target:** Non-selective melanocortin agonist (MC1R/MC3R/MC4R; brain-penetrant); arousal effect primarily via central MC4R in hypothalamic pathways (CNS mechanism distinct from PDE5/vasodilators).
- **Research use cases:** Hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women (approved); historically ED (intranasal halted over BP); appetite/energy-balance interest.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- Phase 3 RECONNECT trials showed significant improvement in desire and reduced distress vs placebo (modest effect sizes). ED studies erectogenic but discontinued (cardiovascular).
- **Side effects:** Nausea (~40%, leading discontinuation cause), flushing, headache, injection-site reactions, transient BP rise with compensatory HR decrease, focal hyperpigmentation/gum darkening with repeated use.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension/known CVD (transient BP rise); limit dosing per label (one/24 h, capped monthly); approved only for premenopausal women (not postmenopausal/men/performance); slows gastric emptying (oral-drug absorption).
- **Handling:** Vyleesi single-use prefilled autoinjector (SC abdomen/thigh); store controlled room temp, dark, do not freeze. Research bremelanotide frozen/desiccated, reconstitute bacteriostatic water, refrigerate, dark, no freeze-thaw.
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved (2019, Vyleesi) for premenopausal HSDD. Melanocortin agonists scrutinized by WADA; no sanctioned sport use.
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB12320; RECONNECT (Obstet Gynecol 2019)

### MT-1 (Afamelanotide; Scenesse; NDP-MSH)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic linear tridecapeptide; potent stabilized alpha-MSH analog (strong MC1R). FDA/EMA-approved orphan drug.
- **Identity:** Ac-Ser-Tyr-Ser-Nle-Glu-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Gly-Lys-Pro-Val-NH2 (Nle4, D-Phe7); ~1646.9 Da; CAS 75921-69-6.
- **Mechanism / target:** MC1R agonist on epidermal melanocytes; stimulates photoprotective eumelanin synthesis independent of UV, increasing pigmentation/photoprotection.
- **Research use cases:** Approved for phototoxicity prevention in erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP); investigated in polymorphic light eruption, vitiligo, photoprotection.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- Phase 3 EPP RCTs: increased pain-free sun exposure and quality of life (basis for approval); measurable melanin increase. Other indications investigational.
- **Side effects:** Generally well tolerated; implant-site reactions, headache, nausea, fatigue, hyperpigmentation/darkening of nevi, melanocytic nevus changes (skin surveillance advised).
- **Warnings/limitations:** Requires regular skin monitoring for nevus changes; caution with melanoma/skin-cancer history; delivered as a controlled-release implant by trained physicians only.
- **Handling:** Scenesse is a sterile bioresorbable SC implant (~16 mg) above the iliac crest -- not a reconstituted injection; store refrigerated 2-8C. Research lyophilizate frozen/desiccated, dark.
- **Regulatory:** EMA-approved (2014), FDA-approved (2019, Scenesse) for EPP -- distinct from unregulated melanotan products. Melanocortin agonists draw WADA attention.
- **Sources:** go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB04931; ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/scenesse


### SNAP-8 (Acetyl Octapeptide-3)
- **Class / type:** Synthetic topical cosmeceutical octapeptide; competitive SNARE inhibitor ("topical neuromodulator"); Argireline extension. Cosmetic ingredient.
- **Identity:** Ac-Glu-Glu-Met-Gln-Arg-Arg-Ala-Asp-NH2; ~889 Da. Mimics SNAP-25 N-terminus.
- **Mechanism / target:** Competitively mimics SNAP-25 N-terminus, destabilizing SNARE (SNAP-25/syntaxin/VAMP) assembly, impairing vesicle fusion at the neuromuscular junction, reducing acetylcholine release and facial muscle contraction. Reversible and far milder than botulinum toxin (which permanently cleaves SNAP-25).
- **Research use cases:** Topical anti-wrinkle/expression-line reduction (forehead/periorbital).
- **Reported benefits:** Manufacturer/cosmetic studies report wrinkle-depth reduction (up to ~63% cited). Largely industry-sponsored, small cohorts; independent peer-reviewed efficacy limited. Topical agent -- not injectable.
- **Side effects:** Low-risk topically; possible mild local irritation/redness/contact sensitivity; no significant systemic toxicity in cosmetic use; stratum-corneum penetration limited (reduces risk and efficacy).
- **Warnings/limitations:** Topical cosmetic use only -- not an injectable or drug; modest, penetration-limited efficacy; not a botulinum substitute; avoid broken/irritated skin; patch-test; limited pregnancy/lactation data.
- **Handling:** Aqueous concentrate or lyophilized powder; store cool/dark; methionine oxidation-sensitive (potency loss); incorporate cold; avoid heat/freeze-thaw.
- **Regulatory:** Cosmetic ingredient (INCI: Acetyl Octapeptide-3); not an FDA drug; no therapeutic claims. Not WADA-relevant.
- **Sources:** biolyphar.com (SNAP-8 chemistry); naturalorganicskincare.com (SNAP-8 vs Argireline)

# 8. Incretin & Fat-Loss Metabolics

> Incretin agents (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, survodutide) carry class GI effects and (for approved/rodent data) thyroid C-cell/MTC and pancreatitis/gallbladder considerations. Approved products are distinct from compounded versions (compounded drugs are not FDA-evaluated).

### Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus; NN9535)
- **Class / type:** Long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist; acylated incretin mimetic. FDA-approved.
- **Identity:** 31-aa GLP-1 analog (Aib2, Arg34, C18 fatty-diacid on Lys26); C187H291N45O59; ~4113.6 g/mol; CAS 910463-68-2. SC bioavailability ~89%; oral (Rybelsus + SNAC) ~1-2%.
- **Mechanism / target:** Selective GLP-1R agonist; glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppressed glucagon, slowed gastric emptying, central appetite/satiety. Albumin binding -> ~1-week half-life (weekly SC).
- **Research use cases:** T2D glycemic control, chronic weight management, CV risk reduction, HFpEF with obesity, MASLD/NASH, CKD in T2D.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- SUSTAIN/PIONEER (T2D); STEP (obesity) ~15% weight loss at 68 wk (2.4 mg); SELECT reduced MACE in overweight/obese with CVD, no diabetes; STEP-HFpEF improved symptoms/function.
- **Side effects:** Dose-dependent GI (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain); gallbladder disease, acute pancreatitis (MHRA monitoring rare severe); hypoglycemia mainly with insulin/sulfonylureas.
- **Warnings/limitations:** FDA boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors (rodent MTC); contraindicated with personal/family MTC or MEN 2; not for type 1 diabetes; caution pancreatitis history and diabetic retinopathy.
- **Handling:** Pens refrigerated 2-8C pre-use; room-temp in-use window. Research lyophilizate desiccated/frozen, reconstitute bacteriostatic water, refrigerate; heat/freeze-thaw/agitation-sensitive. Oral form requires fasting administration with minimal water (SNAC-dependent).
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved (Ozempic 2017, Rybelsus 2019, Wegovy 2021). FDA cautions compounded salt forms (sodium/acetate) not established safe/effective.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semaglutide; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185 (STEP 1); nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563 (SELECT)

### Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound; LY3298176)
- **Class / type:** Dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist ("twincretin"); acylated peptide. FDA-approved.
- **Identity:** 39-aa with C20 fatty-diacid; C225H348N48O68; ~4813.5 g/mol; CAS 2023788-19-2. Weekly SC.
- **Mechanism / target:** Agonist at GIP and GLP-1 receptors; enhances glucose-dependent insulin, suppresses glucagon, slows gastric emptying, reduces appetite; GIP adds complementary metabolic/appetite effects.
- **Research use cases:** T2D, chronic weight management, obstructive sleep apnea in obesity, MASLD/NASH, CV/metabolic endpoints.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- SURPASS (T2D, superior to semaglutide 1 mg in SURPASS-2); SURMOUNT (obesity); SURMOUNT-1 three-year analysis: 94% reduction in progression to T2D in pre-diabetes + obesity; FDA approved Zepbound for moderate-severe OSA in obesity.
- **Side effects:** Dose-dependent GI (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation); class gallbladder/pancreatitis signals (MHRA severe-pancreatitis guidance).
- **Warnings/limitations:** FDA boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors; contraindicated with MTC/MEN 2 history; not for type 1 diabetes; hypoglycemia with insulin/secretagogues.
- **Handling:** Pens/vials refrigerated 2-8C; limited room-temp window. Research lyophilizate frozen/desiccated, reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate; avoid freeze-thaw/heat/shaking.
- **Regulatory:** FDA-approved -- Mounjaro (T2D 2022), Zepbound (obesity 2023; OSA 2024). Approved product distinct from compounded.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirzepatide; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35658024 (SURMOUNT-1); 34370970 (SURPASS-2)

### Retatrutide (LY-3437943; "triple-G")
- **Class / type:** Investigational triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon (GCGR) agonist; acylated peptide.
- **Identity:** SPPS peptide with Aib substitutions, alpha-methyl-leucine, C-terminal serinamide, Lys with AEEA-(gamma-Glu)-(C20 diacid) lipidation; CAS 2381089-83-2; ChEMBL CHEMBL5095485; UNII NOP2Y096GV. Weekly.
- **Mechanism / target:** Co-agonism at GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors; glucagon arm increases energy expenditure and lipolysis/fat oxidation on top of incretin appetite/glucose actions.
- **Research use cases:** Obesity/overweight, T2D, body-composition, MASLD, CVD (TRIUMPH-3); preclinical obesity-associated cancer interest.
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- Phase 2 (Jastreboff, NEJM 2023): up to ~17.5% weight reduction at 24 wk, larger at 48 wk; meta-analyses ~15-24% over 48-72 wk; T2D substudy body-fat differences at 36 wk (Lancet Diab Endo 2025). Preclinical -- reduced tumor engraftment/growth in pancreatic/lung mouse models (exceeding semaglutide in that model). Phase 3 (TRIUMPH) ongoing.
- **Side effects:** Dose-dependent GI (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal discomfort), fatigue, headache, mild HR increases; low hypoglycemia, infrequent serious AEs in non-diabetic trials to date; class pancreatitis/gallbladder/GI risks apply; long-term outcomes not established.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Investigational -- no label, no long-term safety; theoretical thyroid C-cell/pancreatitis considerations under study. Not approved.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store frozen/desiccated, dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate; avoid freeze-thaw/agitation; lipidated/amphiphilic -- gentle reconstitution avoids foaming/aggregation.
- **Regulatory:** Investigational (Eli Lilly); Phase 3 ongoing; not FDA-approved; non-trial material research-use-only.
- **Sources:** pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315 (Jastreboff); 35985340 (Coskun POC); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retatrutide

### Survodutide (BI 456906)
- **Class / type:** Investigational dual GLP-1/glucagon (GCGR) agonist (oxyntomodulin-based); acylated peptide.
- **Identity:** Peptide with C18-diacid albumin-binding side chain; C192H289N47O61; ~4231.7 g/mol; CAS 2805997-46-8; PubChem CID 168429725; UNII 2ALA66NS64. Weekly SC.
- **Mechanism / target:** Balanced GLP-1R (insulin/satiety/slowed emptying) + glucagon-receptor (energy expenditure, hepatic lipid mobilization) co-agonism; glucagon arm underlies hepatic-fat interest.
- **Research use cases:** Obesity, T2D, MASH/NASH (notable hepatic interest).
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- meta-analysis of 3 RCTs (1,088 patients): 4-11 months at 2.4-4.8 mg/wk associated with ~7-9% weight reduction (~9-15 kg); Phase 2 activity in obesity and MASH histologic improvement.
- **Side effects:** Predominantly GI (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), mild/dose-dependent; glucagon agonism can raise heart rate.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Investigational -- no approved indication/long-term safety; class incretin considerations under study. Not approved.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store frozen/desiccated, dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate; avoid freeze-thaw/shaking.
- **Regulatory:** Investigational (Boehringer Ingelheim/Zealand Pharma); not FDA-approved.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survodutide; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38856224 (MASH Phase 2)

### Cagrilintide (AM833)
- **Class / type:** Investigational long-acting amylin analog (dual amylin + calcitonin receptor agonist, DACRA); acylated cyclic peptide.
- **Identity:** Human amylin analog with disulfide ring and C20-diacid; C194H312N54O59S2; CAS 1415456-99-3; PubChem CID 167312356; DrugBank DB18887; UNII AO43BIF1U8. Weekly SC.
- **Mechanism / target:** Amylin + calcitonin receptor agonist; slows gastric emptying, promotes satiety, modulates food intake via hindbrain -- a non-overlapping pathway to incretins.
- **Research use cases:** Obesity and T2D, principally in fixed combination with semaglutide (CagriSema).
- **Reported benefits:** Human -- Phase 2 (Lau, Lancet 2021) established weekly activity; Phase 3 REDEFINE 1: 2.4 mg monotherapy ~11.5-11.8% weight reduction at 68 wk vs ~2.3-3.0% placebo (~1 in 3 reached >=15%); Phase 2 combo with semaglutide (Frias, Lancet 2023).
- **Side effects:** Predominantly GI (nausea, vomiting), mild-moderate/dose-dependent; injection-site reactions.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Investigational -- no approved indication; long-term safety not established. Not approved.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store frozen/desiccated; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate, dark; avoid freeze-thaw/agitation; cyclic disulfide -- avoid reducing conditions.
- **Regulatory:** Investigational (Novo Nordisk); not FDA-approved.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagrilintide; pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34798060 (Lau); go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB18887

### Combination: Cagrilintide + Semaglutide (CagriSema)
Investigational fixed combination (cagrilintide 2.4 mg + semaglutide 2.4 mg) exploiting amylin + incretin synergy via complementary, non-redundant satiety pathways. Phase 1b (Enebo, Lancet 2021) established tolerability/PK; Phase 2 ~-15.6% at 32 wk. Phase 3 REDEFINE (NEJM, June 2025): REDEFINE 1 (68 wk, 3,417, obesity/overweight) -- CagriSema -20.4% vs -11.5% cagrilintide, -14.9% semaglutide, -3.0% placebo; REDEFINE 2 (68 wk, 1,206, T2D) -- -13.7% vs -3.1% placebo. AEs predominantly GI. Investigational (Novo Nordisk); not FDA-approved.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagrilintide/semaglutide; pubmed 40544433 (REDEFINE 1); 40544432 (REDEFINE 2); 33894838 (Enebo)


### MOTS-c (Mitochondrial ORF of 12S rRNA type-c)
- **Class / type:** Mitochondrial-derived peptide (MDP); endogenous 16-aa microprotein; studied as exercise mimetic. Research-only; WADA-banned.
- **Identity:** MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR (16 aa), encoded by MT-RNR1; C101H152N28O22S2; ~2174.6 g/mol; CAS 1627580-64-6; PubChem CID 146675088. Discovered 2015.
- **Mechanism / target:** Regulates metabolic homeostasis; modulates AMPK and folate-methionine/purine metabolism, promoting skeletal-muscle glucose utilization; upregulated by exercise/stress; translocates to nucleus to influence stress-response (Nrf2-related) genes; reported CK2 binding.
- **Research use cases:** Metabolic homeostasis, insulin resistance/obesity, exercise mimetics, aging/healthspan, neurodegenerative/cardiovascular research.
- **Reported benefits:** Animal/in-vitro (Lee, Cell Metab 2015) -- promoted metabolic homeostasis, reduced obesity/insulin resistance in mice. Human -- observational (exercise-induced circulating MOTS-c, Sci Rep 2021). Predominantly preclinical/mechanistic; controlled human therapeutic trials lacking.
- **Side effects:** No well-characterized human AE profile (no formal human safety trials); human safety not established.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not approved; EMPHASIS -- WADA-banned effective 2024 (relevant for competitive athletes); evidence predominantly preclinical.
- **Handling:** Lyophilized; store frozen/desiccated, dark; reconstitute bacteriostatic/sterile water, refrigerate short-term; two methionines (oxidation-prone) -- minimize air exposure, avoid freeze-thaw.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; research-use-only. WADA-prohibited (2024).
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOTS-c; pubmed 25738459 (Lee); usada.org (2024 list changes)

### Lemon Bottle (Riboflavin / Bromelain / Lecithin lipolytic blend)
- **Class / type:** Proprietary cosmetic/aesthetic lipolytic injection blend (localized fat dissolution); not a single molecule.
- **Identity:** Multi-ingredient solution; commonly listed actives -- riboflavin (B2), bromelain (pineapple proteolytic enzyme), lecithin (phosphatidylcholine). No single CAS.
- **Mechanism / target:** Proposed (not rigorously established) local adipocyte membrane disruption/emulsification by phosphatidylcholine, with bromelain proteolytic/anti-inflammatory action and riboflavin as a metabolic cofactor/photosensitive carrier; purported localized lipolysis.
- **Research use cases:** Marketed for non-surgical localized fat reduction (submental, small deposits). Peer-reviewed controlled data very limited.
- **Reported benefits:** Largely manufacturer/practitioner-reported (anecdotal/aesthetic case reports), not large RCTs; low evidence quality.
- **Side effects:** Injectable-lipolytic class -- local swelling, erythema, pain, bruising, nodules, injection-site inflammation; bromelain allergy potential (pineapple/latex cross-sensitivity); limited surveillance.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not an FDA-approved drug; efficacy/safety not regulator-reviewed; EMPHASIS -- evidence largely anecdotal; allergy risk; sterility/compounding quality varies.
- **Handling:** Usually pre-mixed aqueous; riboflavin makes it yellow and photosensitive -- protect from light, store cool per supplier; discard if precipitated/discolored beyond expected tint.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; sold as cosmetic/aesthetic in some markets; no US drug status.
- **Sources:** pubchem (Riboflavin); fda.gov approved-drugs (product not listed)

### LIPO-C (MIC + B-vitamin lipotropic blend)
- **Class / type:** Compounded lipotropic injectable blend (Methionine, Inositol, Choline + B-vitamins, sometimes L-carnitine); not a single molecule.
- **Identity:** Multi-component -- Methionine (sulfur amino acid), Inositol (sugar-alcohol), Choline (methyl donor), Vitamin B12 (cyano-/methylcobalamin); some add L-carnitine. No single CAS.
- **Mechanism / target:** "Lipotropic" -- methionine/choline are methyl donors in hepatic lipid metabolism/phospholipid/VLDL handling; inositol in lipid signaling; B12 in energy metabolism. No specific receptor target; rationale is nutritional/cofactor supplementation.
- **Research use cases:** Marketed as a diet/exercise adjunct for weight-management/"fat metabolism support." Controlled evidence for independent fat loss is weak/lacking.
- **Reported benefits:** Predominantly anecdotal/clinic-marketed; individual nutrients have defined roles (choline deficiency relates to hepatic steatosis) but injectable-blend human weight-loss benefit not robustly established.
- **Side effects:** Injection-site reactions, transient GI upset, rare hypersensitivity; high-dose methionine/choline metabolic considerations; generally low-risk at typical compounded amounts but lacks rigorous safety-trial data.
- **Warnings/limitations:** Not FDA-approved; compounded (quality/content varies); EMPHASIS -- fat-loss efficacy not established by controlled trials; caution B12/cobalt sensitivity and renal impairment.
- **Handling:** Usually pre-mixed aqueous; B12 imparts pink/red color and is light-sensitive -- protect from light, refrigerate per pharmacy; discard if cloudy/precipitated.
- **Regulatory:** Not FDA-approved; pharmacy-compounded (not FDA-evaluated). Research-use-only in this context.
- **Sources:** pubchem (Choline, Methionine); ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional; fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding

# 9. Reconstitution Supplies

### Bacteriostatic Water (0.9% benzyl-alcohol-preserved Sterile Water)
- Sterile water for injection containing ~0.9% benzyl alcohol as a bacteriostatic preservative, allowing multi-dose use over a limited period (commonly up to ~28 days refrigerated once opened). The standard diluent for reconstituting most lyophilized peptides in this catalog. Benzyl alcohol is contraindicated in neonates and should be used cautiously in large volumes. Store at controlled room temperature; keep sterile.

### Acetic Acid 0.6% (dilute acetic acid diluent)
- A weakly acidic aqueous diluent used to reconstitute peptides that are poorly soluble in plain or bacteriostatic water (e.g., some long/hydrophobic peptides such as IGF-1 LR3). The low pH improves solubility and short-term stability of certain peptides; it is not bacteriostatically preserved, so reconstituted material is typically aliquoted and frozen. Use only the concentration/diluent a given peptide's documentation specifies.

## WADA-Prohibited (verify current list)

All GH secretagogues/GHRH analogs (CJC-1295 +/-DAC, Sermorelin, Tesamorelin, GHRP-2/6, Hexarelin, Ipamorelin), all GH/IGF/anabolic agents (Somatropin, HGH Frag 176-191, AOD9604, IGF-1 LR3, Follistatin), TB-500/Tbeta4, AICAR, MOTS-c. HCG/HMG prohibited in males. BPC-157/KPV fall under WADA S0 (non-approved-substance) catch-all.

## Notable Safety Flags

- **VIP/Aviptadil:** infusion hypotension; severe secretory diarrhea at high dose.
- **IGF-1 LR3:** hypoglycemia and proliferative/neoplasia caveat amplified by long half-life.
- **Incretins:** thyroid C-cell/MTC boxed warning, pancreatitis/gallbladder signals.
- **HGH:** acromegaly with overdose; contraindicated in malignancy/critical illness.
- **Pro-angiogenic peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu/AHK-Cu):** theoretical tumor-vascularization caveat, compounded in stacks.
- **IV NAD+ / IV glutathione:** infusion reactions; compounded, non-approved.
