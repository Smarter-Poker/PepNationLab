# Intranasal (Nasal Spray) Viability Of The PepNationLab Catalog

> Research-use-only reference. Last compiled: 2026-06-14.
> Purpose: identify which catalog compounds can realistically be delivered by the
> intranasal route instead of injection, graded by the strength of the actual
> evidence. Every verdict is tied to published literature, an approved/registered
> product, or a clearly stated absence of evidence.

## How To Read This

A "nasal spray" SKU being sold online is a marketing decision, NOT proof the nasal
route works. The single most important rule applied throughout: **a commercial nasal
product existing does not equal evidence of nasal absorption or efficacy.** The gap
between "sold as a spray" and "proven to work as a spray" is widest for BPC-157,
TB-500, SS-31, NAD+, HGH Fragment 176-191, and the GLP-1 weight-loss peptides.

The other governing rule is molecular size. Nasal mucosa absorbs small peptides
(roughly under ~1,500 Da) reasonably well; absorption falls off sharply above that
and is negligible for large glycoproteins (HCG, HMG, Follistatin) and the
~4,000-5,000 Da GLP-1 class.

### Verdict Tiers

- **ESTABLISHED** - The intranasal route is well documented: an approved or
  nationally registered nasal product exists, and/or there is solid human clinical
  or research use via the nose. These are the genuine "nasal instead of injection"
  candidates.
- **EMERGING / PLAUSIBLE** - Real published intranasal research (often animal models)
  or a strong size/mechanism rationale exists, but it is not a standard, validated
  human route. Promising, not proven.
- **NOT SUITABLE** - Injection-only in practice. Either too large to cross nasal
  mucosa, or zero credible nasal evidence, or the validated/approved route is
  injection.

## The One Big Warning To Surface Loudly

Your most popular weight-loss injectables - **Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide,
Survodutide, and Cagrilintide** - are all ~4,100-5,000 Da acylated peptides and are
**NOT viable as nasal sprays.** A researcher assuming they can swap their injectable
GLP-1 for an equivalent nasal spray would be mistaken. Any "GLP-1 / tirzepatide /
retatrutide nasal spray" sold anywhere is a gray-market product with no validated
pharmacokinetics. Oral semaglutide (Rybelsus) only works with a special SNAC
absorption enhancer plus large dose compensation - nasal delivery has not solved that
problem commercially.
