Lesson 1 Of 10 - 4 Minutes

What Exactly Is A Peptide?

Imagine You Have LEGO Blocks. Tiny Colored Bricks. You Take 2, 10, Or 50 Of These Blocks And Stack Them Together. That's A Peptide. It's Just A Short Chain Of Building Blocks Connected Together. The Building Blocks Have A Special Name: Amino Acids.

The Simple Rule

2 To 50 Building Blocks = Peptide

51+ Building Blocks = Protein

That's It. Size Is The Only Difference. Your Body Makes Both All The Time.

The Three Main Things Peptides Do

1
They Send Messages

Like A Text Message For Your Cells. They Tell Cells: "Hey, Do This!"

2
They Don't Last Long

Your Body Breaks Them Down Fast. In Hours, Not Days. This Is Good Because Your Body Stays In Control.

3
Your Body Makes Them

Right Now, Your Body Is Making Peptides. Scientists Often Copy The Ones Your Body Already Makes.

Peptides You Already Know

Insulin

A Peptide That Controls Your Blood Sugar. Makes It Go Down When You Eat.

Happy Chemicals

Your Brain Makes These. Released When You Laugh, Exercise, Or Feel Good.

Key Fact: Your Body Has Trillions Of Cells. Peptides Are Like The Messages Your Body Uses To Tell Cells What To Do.

How Do Building Blocks Connect?

When Two Amino Acid Blocks Want To Connect, They Hold Hands Really Tight. This Connection Has A Fancy Name: Peptide Bond. It's Like Super Glue Between LEGO Bricks.

What Happens When They Connect

Block A Says: "Can I Hold Your Hand?"

Block B Says: "Yes!"

A Tiny Bit Of Water Gets Released. (It's No Big Deal.)

Now They're Connected Forever. (Until Your Body Breaks Them Apart.)

The Two Ends

Every Peptide Chain Has A Start And An End:

N-Terminus (The Start)

Where The Chain Begins. Written First In Scientific Names.

C-Terminus (The End)

Where The Chain Ends. Written Last In Scientific Names.

Lesson 5 Of 10 - 6 Minutes

How Do You Keep Peptides Safe?

Peptides Are Fragile. Like A Snowflake. If You Don't Keep Them Comfortable, They Break Apart And Stop Working. Scientists Need To Be Very Careful.

The Easy Rule

Cold. Dark. Dry.

That's It. Keep Peptides: Cold (Like In A Fridge Or Freezer). Dark (Away From Light). Dry (Not Wet).

Powder Vs. Liquid

Dry Powder

Lasts A Really Long Time. Months Or Even Years If You Keep It Cold And Dark.

Liquid Solution

Doesn't Last As Long. Days Or Weeks. You Need To Use It Before It Goes Bad.

The Big Problem: Freeze-Thaw

If You Take A Peptide Out Of The Freezer And Thaw It, Then Freeze It Again, It Gets Damaged. It's Like Crumpling And Uncrimpling A Piece Of Paper Over And Over. Eventually It Tears.

Fix: Use Small Portions So You Never Thaw A Big Batch Twice.

Why? Peptides Are Made Of Atoms Held Together By Tiny Electric Forces. Heat, Light, And Water Make These Forces Weaker Until The Peptide Falls Apart.

Different Peptide Families

Just Like You Have A Family With Cousins And Siblings, Peptides Have Families Too. They're Grouped By What They Do And How They Work.

Growth Peptides

These Tell Your Body To Grow Bigger And Stronger. Scientists Study Them To Understand How Growth Happens.

Example: GHRP (A Peptide That Signals Growth)

Brain Peptides

These Send Messages In Your Brain. They Can Affect Mood, Memory, And How You Feel.

Example: Happy Chemicals (Peptides Your Brain Makes)

Metabolism Peptides

These Tell Your Body How To Use Energy And Control Your Appetite. They Help Manage Blood Sugar.

Example: GLP-1 (A Peptide Made In Your Gut)

Repair Peptides

These Help Your Body Fix Damage And Heal Wounds. They Signal Your Body To Build New Tissue.

Example: Anti-Aging Peptides (Studied For Healing)

Stacking And Lab Protocols

Scientists Sometimes Use Multiple Peptides Together. This Is Called Stacking. Each One Does A Different Job, And Together They Work Better.

What Is Stacking?

Imagine Building A Tower With LEGO. You Don't Use Just One Color. You Mix Colors To Make It Stronger And Look Better. Stacking Is The Same Idea.

Lab Protocols

A Protocol Is A Recipe For Scientists. It Says: Do This First. Then Do That. Then Do This. Following The Recipe Exactly Makes Sure The Experiment Works Right.

Key Rules For Lab Work

Keep Everything Super Clean

Measure Everything Exactly

Follow The Steps In Order

Document Everything You Do

Remember: Following Protocols Exactly Is How Scientists Make Sure Results Are Real And Can Be Repeated By Other Scientists.
Lesson 8 Of 10 - 4 Minutes

Making A Solution: Reconstitution

Most Peptides Come As Dry Powder. To Use Them, Scientists Add Special Water To Make A Liquid. This Is Called Reconstitution. It Just Means: "Make It Liquid Again."

The Steps (Simple)

1

Get The Powder Vial And Special Water

2

Slowly Add Water To The Powder

3

Gently Swirl. Don't Shake Hard.

4

Done! Now You Have A Solution

Why Not Shake Hard?

If You Shake It Really Hard, You Make Bubbles. Bubbles Can Damage The Peptide. Think Of It Like Shaking A Soda Can Too Much—The Foam Ruins It.

After Reconstitution

Once You Add Water, Use The Peptide Soon. Keep It In The Fridge. It Doesn't Last Very Long As A Liquid. Think Of It Like Fresh Orange Juice—Use It Fast Before It Goes Bad.

Lesson 9 Of 10 - 3 Minutes

Dosing Reference Guide

When Scientists Use Peptides In Labs, They Need To Know The Right Amount To Use. This Is Called A Dose. Different Peptides Need Different Amounts.

How Doses Work

Just Like A Medicine Has Instructions ("Take Two Pills"), Peptides Have Doses. The Dose Depends On: What Peptide It Is. What You're Testing. How Big Your Sample Is.

Why Dose Matters

Too Little Peptide

Nothing Happens. The Signal Is Too Weak.

Just Right Amount

The Signal Works. You See The Effect.

Too Much Peptide

Might Break Things. Or Stop Working Right.

Important:

This Is Only For Lab Work. Never Use Peptides Outside Of Labs. Never Give Them To People Or Animals. This Information Is For Learning Only.
