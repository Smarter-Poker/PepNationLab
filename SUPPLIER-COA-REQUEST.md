# Supplier COA Request

Send this to your synthesis facility. Attach or paste the list of lot numbers
you have on hand, if you have them; if you do not, ask them to key off your
purchase orders.

---

**Subject:** Certificates Of Analysis For Delivered Batches

Hello,

We are formalizing our batch documentation and need the Certificates Of
Analysis for every lot you have shipped us to date, plus a COA accompanying
every future shipment as standard.

For each lot, please send:

1. The signed COA as a PDF, showing the lot or batch number exactly as it
   appears on the vial labels you shipped.
2. The HPLC purity result, with the method stated (column, mobile phase,
   detection wavelength).
3. The mass spectrometry result: observed mass against theoretical mass.
4. Water content and net peptide content, if determined.
5. The name of the laboratory that performed the testing, and whether it is
   your in-house lab or an independent third party.
6. The raw chromatogram, if you retain it.

If any of these were not measured for a given lot, please say so explicitly
rather than omitting the line. We publish exactly what was tested, and we mark
untested parameters as not reported.

Two questions:

- Do you retain reference samples from each batch? If so, we would like the
  option to send retains for independent third-party verification.
- Is your testing performed in-house, or by an accredited external laboratory?
  If external, which one, and are they ISO/IEC 17025 accredited?

Please send everything you have on file. Where a COA does not exist for a lot
we have already received, tell us plainly rather than producing one
retroactively.

Thank you,

Dan
Pep Nation Lab

## If The Supplier Cannot Produce COAs

Two things follow.

First, this is the most important thing you will learn about your supply chain
this quarter. A manufacturer that releases peptide batches without analytical
testing does not know what is in the vials either.

Second, you can test independently. Send retain vials from your highest-volume
products to a third-party analytical laboratory. Janoshik Analytical is the one
most commonly used by peptide vendors and the one buyers in this market
recognize; turnaround is typically a few days and cost is roughly fifty to
ninety dollars per sample. Start with the products that move the most units.

Upload whatever comes back through the admin lot screen, verify it against the
signed PDF, and the certificate goes live on `/coa`.

## A Note On The About Page

`/about` currently states that every batch is tested with full COA
documentation available for review. Until certificates are on file, that
sentence describes an intention rather than a fact. Either the certificates
arrive and it becomes true, or the wording should describe the sourcing
standard rather than assert completed testing. This is worth resolving before
the COA pages go live, because the two will be read together.
